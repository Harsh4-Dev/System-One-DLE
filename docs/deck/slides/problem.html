The Problem

The big model is not always right

58.1%

Laya (395M-parameter encoder) on Emotion tweets, zero-shot. A 1.2M-parameter Bi-LSTM gets 92.2%.

Big models are slow

635×

slower on a CPU: Laya takes 706 ms per news article, a TextCNN 1.1 ms.

Confidence is not honest

0.214

calibration error (ECE) of Laya’s fine-tuned checkpoint on the typed-decisions benchmark: its confidence is off by about 21 points.

What this shows: no single model wins on accuracy, cost and honesty at once, so the right choice depends on the input. The course asks us to pick the right architecture for an application; ADAR makes that choice for every input.

Three problems, all measured in our own run. First, the biggest model is not always the best: Laya gets only 58.1% on emotion tweets, while a 1.2 million parameter Bi-LSTM from our syllabus gets 92.2%. Second, the big model is expensive: on a CPU it is 635× slower than a TextCNN. Third, confidence is not honest: Laya's fine-tuned checkpoint has a calibration error of 0.214 on the typed-decisions benchmark. Our course objective is to identify the right architecture for an application. We asked whether a model can learn to make that choice per input.
