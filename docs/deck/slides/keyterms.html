Before we start

Key Terms

System-One model

Answers typed questions (choice, score, yes/no) in one forward pass, with a probability for every option. No text generation.

Expert

A small syllabus network trained for the task: TextCNN, RNN, LSTM or GRU. On a CPU it takes 1.1 ms–6.4 ms per input.

Out-of-fold

We only use an expert’s opinion on data that expert never trained on, so its confidence is not inflated.

Calibration (ECE)

Does “80% sure” mean right 80% of the time? ECE is the average gap. Lower is better; 0 is perfect.

Log-loss (NLL)

Punishes confident mistakes hardest. Lower means the probabilities are more honest, not just the answers more often right.

Cascade

Cheap experts answer first; only inputs they are unsure about “escalate” to the expensive model.

What this sets up: we judge every model on two things, how often it is right and how honest its confidence is.

A few terms we use throughout. A System-One model answers typed questions in a single pass, giving a probability for every option instead of generating text. Experts are our small syllabus networks. Out-of-fold means we only trust an expert's opinion on data it never trained on. Calibration asks whether 80 percent sure really means right 80 percent of the time; the expected calibration error, ECE, is the average gap. Log-loss punishes confident mistakes. A cascade lets cheap models answer first and escalates only the hard inputs. So every model is judged on two things: how often it is right, and how honest its confidence is.
