Each expert trains 3 times and only predicts the third of the data it never saw. The head learns from those honest opinions.

2 · A loss that rewards honesty

L = −log p_y
+ Σ_k (p_k − y_k)²

Log score + Brier score. Both are lowest only when the probabilities are honest: proper scoring rules, the same idea Laya is trained with.

3 · Calibrate

p = softmax(z / T)

One temperature T is fitted on validation data, so “80% sure” means right about 80% of the time.

What this shows: every training choice targets honest probabilities, not only accuracy; the router and the leaderboard work depend on that.

Three ideas make the head trustworthy. One: honest expert opinions. Each expert is trained three times, each time leaving out a third of the data, and we keep only its predictions on the third it never saw; otherwise the head would learn to trust experts that memorised their training data. Two: the loss adds the log score and the Brier score. Both are proper scoring rules: the only way to minimise them is to report your true belief, the same principle behind Laya's own training. Three: after training we fit one temperature on validation data, so that 80 percent confidence really means right 80 percent of the time.
