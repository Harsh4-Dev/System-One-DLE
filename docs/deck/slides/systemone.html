What this shows: a System-One model already gives a probability for every option. ADAR needs exactly that to weigh Laya against the small networks.

A System-One model is named after fast, intuitive System 1 thinking. Instead of writing text word by word like a chatbot, it reads a state, here a customer message, plus typed questions, and answers all of them in one forward pass: a probability for every option, a level on a scale, or a yes/no probability called noul. TypeSafe released the first one, Jev. We use Laya, the open-source alternative: a 395 million parameter ModernBERT-large encoder with a decision head, Apache licensed. Because it outputs probabilities, ADAR can weigh it against other models.
